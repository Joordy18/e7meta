Every hero page is built from numbers the game itself publishes in its public strategy guide. We collect them once, keep a copy for each week, and show them here. Nothing on a hero page is our opinion: it is what players wear, not what we think is best.

## Two sources, two periods

A hero page has two blocks, and each says where it comes from and which dates it covers.

- **Source: gear ranking** lists the sets and artifacts the game ranks for that hero. The period is a ranking week.
- **Source: hunts** comes from the game's hunt statistics: the most worn **set combinations**, the most worn artifacts among those players, and the spread of their stats. It has its own period, which usually starts a week later.

The two periods do not line up, so we never add their numbers together. If two percentages for the same artifact differ between blocks, that is expected: they measure different groups of players.

## Sets and artifacts

The percentage next to a set or an artifact is the share the game's ranking gives it for that hero. The first row is gold: it is the most worn one. A hero with a very high first row (say 90%) has an obvious choice; a hero with a flat list has several builds that players use.

**Set combinations** pair two sets, such as Immunity + Riposte. The game lists a pair in both orders, so we add the two together and show one build. That merge is our own inference: a four-piece set and a two-piece set can only be worn in one layout. It is flagged in the code, and it is the number to doubt first if a combination looks too strong.

## Stat targets

The table in the Stats panel turns each stat's spread into numbers you can aim for.

- **Typical** is the range where most players are.
- **Median** is the range that splits players in half.
- **Top 25% from** is where the best-equipped quarter of players starts. Reach it and you beat three players out of four.
- **Top 10% from** is where the best-equipped tenth starts.

Aim for the **Top 25% from** column as a solid build and for **Top 10% from** as a strong one. The histograms under the table show the same data as bars; the gold bar is the most common range.

> The game only gives each stat as ten buckets between a minimum and a maximum. We assume the buckets are equal in width, so every range in the table is an estimate, rounded to whole numbers.

## What these numbers are not

They describe what players have, not what wins. A popular set can be popular because it is easy to build. Use the numbers to see what is common and what a well-geared hero looks like, then check [the game modes page](#/modes) to see where the hero is actually used.
